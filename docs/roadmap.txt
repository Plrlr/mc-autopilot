MC-AUTOPILOT ROADMAP: OVERWORLD TO ENDER DRAGON
Recommendations for the improvement loop (written September 26, 2026)

HOW TO USE THIS FILE
- Save it in the repo as docs/roadmap.txt and mention it in CLAUDE.md so every session reads it.
- Work top to bottom. Don't start a stage until the previous one is reliable
  (reached on at least 3 of 4 benchmark seeds).
- Make one focused change per cycle so you know what helped.
- If a game fact here disagrees with our Minecraft version, trust the game and note the correction here.


======================================================================
PART 1: LOOP RULES (apply to every stage)
======================================================================

1. Verify milestones from world state, never from the bot's own report.
   Use inventory contents, blocks in the world, advancements, and entity death events.

2. Batch scoreboard, per seed:
   - milestones reached and the time each was reached
   - deaths and their causes
   - time breakdown: food, crafting, mining, travel, combat, idle/stuck
   - top failure reasons from skill results

3. Accept a change only if the target stage improves across the seed set (median time down or
   success rate up) and no earlier stage gets worse. Otherwise revert it.

4. Seeds: keep 4 fixed benchmark seeds for comparisons. Every ~5 batches, add 1-2 fresh seeds
   to catch overfitting to the benchmark seeds.

5. Two kinds of runs:
   - Full runs (honest): no commands, no /locate, no /give. Only these numbers go in the README.
   - Scenario runs (practice): the harness uses commands to start the bot at a later stage
     (for example, in the Nether with iron gear near a fortress). Use these to iterate fast on
     late-game skills. Always label them as scenarios.

6. Code does mechanics; the brain chooses. Anything precise (bucket casting, bridging, crystal
   shooting, triangulation) must be a deterministic coded skill. The AI brain only picks which
   skill runs next.

7. Every skill has: preconditions, a postcondition check from world state, a timeout, a max
   retry count, and a specific failure reason. "Timed out" alone is not a reason.

8. Difficulty: blazes and endermen don't spawn on Peaceful, so the full game is impossible there.
   Benchmark on Easy or Normal, pick one, and keep it fixed.

9. GitHub Actions jobs stop after 6 hours. Checkpoint the world folder and run state at each
   milestone (artifact or cache) and resume in the next job. Artifacts on a public repo can be
   downloaded by anyone, so never include .env or keys.

10. Usage and cost: Opus 5.5 at Medium effort for batch cycles; High only when diagnosing
    something stuck. Set a hard cap on cycles per day and a dollar cap on the Claude credits.
    Log the cost of every cycle.


======================================================================
PART 2: CORE SURVIVAL SYSTEMS (fix these before chasing iron)
======================================================================

FOOD
- Eat only when food <= 14, or <= 17 when health isn't full (natural regeneration needs
  food >= 18). Normal food can't be eaten at full hunger; calling eat then just times out.
- Batch it: hunt 3-5 animals in one trip, then cook everything in one furnace with one fuel load.
- Carry the crafting table and furnace. Mine them back up after use and reuse them. Never place
  a new one if one is in inventory or within 32 blocks.
- Villages: hay bales give 9 wheat each, and bread takes 3 wheat. Villages also have beds and
  chests, sometimes with iron.
- Fuel: coal ore near the surface, or charcoal (smelt logs).

BEDS AND NIGHT
- A bed is 3 wool (sheep) + 3 planks. Sleep to skip the night and set spawn.
- Never use beds in the Nether or the End; they explode.
- No bed at night: work underground or wall the bot in.

DEATHS
- Log the cause from the death chat message.
- After respawning, go back for items only if it's safe. Dropped items despawn after 5 minutes.

STUCK DETECTION
- No position change or no progress for N seconds: cancel the skill, log where and why, and
  pick a different option.

INVENTORY
- Fixed hotbar layout (sword, pickaxe, axe, blocks, food, bucket, and so on).
- Drop junk (extra dirt, diorite, granite, andesite) when free slots fall below 5.

MINING SAFETY (everywhere)
- Never dig straight down or straight up. Use staircases.
- Before breaking a block, check its neighbors for lava and water, and check above for gravel
  or sand.
- Pathfinder settings: avoid lava, limit the safe drop height, allow pillaring with cheap blocks,
  and don't break blocks that would release water or lava onto the path.

PLUGINS TO EVALUATE
- mineflayer-pathfinder, mineflayer-collectblock, mineflayer-tool, mineflayer-auto-eat,
  mineflayer-armor-manager, mineflayer-pvp.
- Check that each one is maintained and supports our Minecraft version before depending on it.


======================================================================
PART 3: STAGES
======================================================================

STAGE 1: WOOD AND STONE (target: stone tools in <= 2 minutes; already reached)
- About 16 logs: planks, sticks, crafting table, wooden pickaxe, then cobblestone for a stone
  pickaxe, axe, sword, and shovel.
- Also collect 8 cobblestone for a furnace and 32+ more for building and pillaring.

STAGE 2: STABLE FOOD (target: food never below 10 for a full 10-minute run)
- Apply the FOOD rules in Part 2.

STAGE 3: IRON (target: 14+ iron ingots)
- Where: since the 1.18 update, iron ore is most common around Y=16 and is also abundant high
  up in mountains. It's often exposed in cave walls.
- How: staircase down to about Y=16, then strip-mine with a 2-high main tunnel and branches every
  3 blocks, mining every visible ore. Or explore caves (faster but riskier). Test both and keep
  whichever has the better median time.
- Spending priority:
  1. iron pickaxe (3 iron)
  2. bucket (3 iron)
  3. shield (1 iron + 6 planks)
  4. flint and steel (1 iron + flint from gravel)
  5. armor: chestplate (8), leggings (7), helmet (5), boots (4)
  6. a second bucket (3) and shears (2) later
- Collect coal on the way and smelt in batches.
- From now on, keep the shield in the offhand and use it to block arrows and creeper blasts.

STAGE 4: NETHER PORTAL (target: portal lit)
- Route A, casting (our "cast" scenario): find a lava pool (surface, caves, or ravines). Pour
  water over lava SOURCE blocks to make obsidian in the shape of a 4-wide by 5-tall frame
  (corners are optional, so 10 obsidian). Water on a source makes obsidian; water on flowing
  lava makes cobblestone, so target sources only. Pick the water back up afterward.
- Route B: mine obsidian with a diamond pickaxe (diamonds are most common deep, around Y -58).
  Slower. Use it only if diamonds turn up anyway.
- Ruined portals often have obsidian and a loot chest (which may include flint and steel,
  obsidian, or gold). Check them whenever one is spotted.
- Light the portal with flint and steel. Save the portal's coordinates.

STAGE 5: NETHER PREP CHECKLIST (don't enter without these)
- At least an iron chestplate (full iron is better), shield, iron sword, iron pickaxe.
- At least one piece of gold armor: golden boots (4 gold) or golden helmet (5 gold). Piglins
  attack players who aren't wearing any. Gold sources: ruined portal chests, gold ore (deep
  underground, and plentiful in badlands), or nether gold ore (drops nuggets; 9 nuggets make
  1 ingot).
- 64+ cobblestone. Ghast fireballs don't break it, but they do break netherrack.
- 16+ cooked food, flint and steel (ghasts can put out the portal), and a crafting table.
- Water evaporates in the Nether, so don't rely on the water bucket there.

STAGE 6: BLAZE RODS (target: 7-8 rods)
- Find a Nether fortress (nether brick). Explore at a steady height, stay away from lava lakes,
  and bridge with cobblestone.
- Fight blazes near their spawner: block fireballs with the shield, fight from cover (a corner or
  a cobblestone wall with a gap), and attack when they come close. Retreat and eat when
  health <= 12.
- Each rod makes 2 blaze powder, so 7-8 rods cover 12-15 eyes of ender.
- Nether distances are 1/8 of Overworld distances. Record the return portal's coordinates.

STAGE 7: ENDER PEARLS (target: 12-15 pearls)
- Best Nether spot: the warped forest biome, which is full of endermen. Overworld nights also
  work but are slower.
- Fighting endermen: stand in a 2-block-high space (they're about 3 blocks tall and can't fit)
  and hit their legs. In the Overworld, standing next to water also helps. Avoid looking at
  endermen you don't intend to fight.
- Alternative: bartering gold with piglins. Pearls are a rare trade and it needs a lot of gold,
  so only try this if gold is plentiful.

STAGE 8: EYES AND STRONGHOLD (target: standing in the end portal room)
- Craft eyes of ender: blaze powder + ender pearl. Aim for 12-15. Some portal frames already
  have eyes (each frame has a 10% chance), and a thrown eye breaks about 20% of the time.
- In Java Edition, the three nearest strongholds are in a ring 1,280-2,816 blocks from the
  world origin.
- Bot advantage, triangulate exactly: throw an eye and record its entity position over a few
  ticks to get a precise bearing. Move 200+ blocks sideways, throw again, compute where the two
  lines cross, and travel there directly. Throw once more near the target; when the eye dives
  downward, the bot is above the stronghold.
- Optional speedup: travel through the Nether (1/8 the distance), then build a new portal near
  the stronghold.
- Staircase down, then navigate corridors to the portal room (it has a silverfish spawner and
  lava under the portal frame). Kill or avoid silverfish.
- Place a bed near the portal room (in the Overworld) so respawns are close.
- Fill every empty frame. The portal activates when all 12 have eyes.

STAGE 9: DRAGON PREP CHECKLIST
- Full iron armor (diamond if available), shield, best sword.
- Bow (3 sticks + 3 string from spiders) and 64 arrows (flint + stick + feather; feathers come
  from chickens; skeletons also drop arrows).
- 64+ cobblestone for pillaring, a water bucket for fall landings, 16+ food, a spare pickaxe.
- Option to test: wear a carved pumpkin (a pumpkin cut with shears). Endermen won't get angry
  when the bot looks at them. The bot doesn't need to "see," so it costs nothing except the
  helmet slot. Compare runs with and without it.

STAGE 10: DRAGON FIGHT (target: the "Free the End" advancement)
- The bot arrives on an obsidian platform. If it's over the void, bridge carefully to the main
  island. Falling into the void is an instant loss, so stay away from edges.
- Destroy all end crystals first; they heal the dragon. There are 10 pillars, and 2 of them are
  caged with iron bars. Shoot crystals with arrows from range. If pillaring up to a crystal,
  never break it in melee from right next to it; it explodes.
- Then the dragon: when it perches on the exit portal (the bedrock fountain in the center),
  attack its head with the sword. While it's flying, use arrows.
- Stay out of the purple dragon's breath clouds. The dragon can knock the bot upward, so use
  the water bucket to land or pillar down carefully.
- Avoid fighting endermen on the island if possible.
- Verify the win from the game: the dragon's death event or the "Free the End" advancement.
